# Load test, 1 October 2026

The 2,000-request run behind the README's results. Everything here was measured on the Gemini free tier, against the Docker stack (`docker compose up`) on a laptop.

**How it was run**

```sh
# .env: CLASSIFIER_MODEL=gemini-3.1-flash-lite   (its own free quota, separate from the answers' model)
uv run python -m loadtest.run --url http://localhost:8001 --requests 2000 --clear-cache \
    --rps 1.5 --llm-rpm 12 --llm-calls-per-miss 1 --llm-budget 440
```

* **Answers:** `gemini-3.5-flash-lite`. **Classifier:** `gemini-3.1-flash-lite`. **Embeddings:** `gemini-embedding-001` (768 dimensions).
* **Workload:** seed 7. 159 questions ("families") in 419 wordings, asked with a Zipf long tail; 96 creative requests (never cached) and 50 weather requests (cached for 5 minutes).
* **Pacing:** at most 12 LLM answer calls a minute, under the free tier's 15. The runner also held back requests that would have been hits while it waited, so the 31 minutes say nothing about throughput.
* **Wrong answers:** re-scored after the run from the server's lookup log, which records the cached question that served each hit (all 1,735 hits paired with their logged lookups, with identical similarities). The first version of the report guessed from the answer text and over-counted (14), because different reviews share the answer "This review is negative."
* **Live feedback during the run:** 17 near misses were labelled by hand in the playground (16 weather questions). After the 10th label, the time-sensitive threshold was learned: **0.97 → 0.93** from about 10:27, shown in the dashboard's "Similarity thresholds" panel. Weather lookups went from 33% hits (30 lookups) to 65% (20 lookups): same-city rewordings at 0.954–0.965 started to hit, while different cities (0.90–0.92) were still rejected. The samples are small, and weather entries also expire after 5 minutes.
* **Classifier:** 275 calls, 0 fallbacks. Its retry rescued 4 brief Google errors (three 429s, one 503).
* **Embeddings:** 420 API calls; 1,599 lookups reused a remembered embedding.
